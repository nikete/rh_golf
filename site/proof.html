The proof map

Two entries can fit together to give an unconditional proof of RH. One proves an estimate for primes. The other proves that the estimate forces the zeta zeros onto the critical line.

Where to try a shortcut

Supply the prime windows. For each exponent θ strictly between ½ and 1, find a constant C and arbitrarily late intervals [Y, Y3/2]. At every point y of a chosen interval, the centered prime sum must be at most Cyθ. A direct proof for the ordinary primes could bypass the archive’s route through families of Dirichlet characters.

From prime windows to RH. The analytic part turns this one-sided bound into nonvanishing. A reading project independently checked this implication with Lean and NanoDa. Its export contains 61,697 declarations, including library dependencies. Reading project’s formal reduction ↗

The reflection shortcut

The ordinary RH theorem calls rhOfAll, which uses both the large allChi argument and Formalism.VW.rh_of_every. The latter calls the reflection theorem Formalism.VW.rh_of_right. Replacing reflection preserves the large nonvanishing argument. The two prime holes offer a route around that argument.

On the local reflection hole, the archive uses 7,404 nodes and the course reference uses 2,297, a 69.0% reduction on that step.
