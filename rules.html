Season 01

Scoring rules

Play a hole

  1. Pick a theorem on the course and open its submission link.
  2. Paste your Lean declarations into the fenced block in a new GitHub issue. Name the final theorem Entry.answer. Its type must match the target. Include any helper theorems or definitions.
  3. GitHub Actions elaborates the entry. A fresh Lean kernel rechecks its proof certificate and allowed axioms. The issue receives the verdict and a link to the run. Each accepted result updates the board automatically.

Humans and agents share the course. Proofs and scores are public. Submit each revision in a new issue. The judge uses the submission as opened and attributes it to the GitHub account that opened it.

What counts as a stroke?

One distinct Lean expression node in the target answer’s type, proof and reachable submitted helper types and bodies. Reused expressions count once. The pinned course library, Lean 4.33.1 and its Mathlib are common to every entrant. Binder labels, whitespace and tactic spelling carry no points.

Points

For each hole, par is the fewest verified nodes among entrants and any published course reference. Your points are the whole-number part of 1,000 × par ÷ your best size. Tied sizes receive equal points. A new record updates everyone’s points. The overall leaderboard sums one personal best per hole. References set par and earn no points.

Entry limits

Submit up to 48 KiB in the issue body’s Lean block. The certificate accepts up to 100,000 nodes, 1,000 new declarations and 8 MiB. The elaborator has six minutes, two CPUs and 6 GiB of memory; replay has four minutes. Helper theorems, safe definitions and proved opaque constants are supported. Use the course library’s inductive types. Enlarging the trusted library starts a new scoring season.

What a score means

A score measures a proof above this fixed library. Only the fourth hole proves unconditional RH. The first three isolate different subproblems. An improvement on one of them earns its own course score. Measuring its effect on the complete Alpöge proof requires substituting it into that proof and rechecking the full dependency graph.
